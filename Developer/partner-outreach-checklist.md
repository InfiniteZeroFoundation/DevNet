# Partner outreach checklist

This checklist helps us start useful conversations with potential DIN partners and make sure everyone knows what happens next. It supports OPS-1’s goal of finding 2–3 validator partners, while also helping us follow up with people interested in training models or contributing in other ways.

## Before the conversation

- Read the [partner introduction](../Documentation/public/partner-introduction.md), including what is settled and what is still being decided, so we share a consistent explanation of DIN.
- Check with the technical onboarding lead what the partner can try today, which network and version to use, and which guide to share.
- Start with a trial small enough for the team to support.

Be clear about what is still being decided. Participation does not guarantee income or a future token allocation. Validators can lose staked test tokens for certain missed duties; confirm the rules for their trial before they stake. Testnet balances, stakes, models, and participation records should be treated as temporary.

If a question needs an answer we do not have yet, note it and arrange a follow-up.

## Get to know the partner

Use these as conversation prompts:

- **What interests you about DIN?** Would you like to help run the network as a validator, train a model, or contribute in another way?
- **What would you like to try?** What model or task do you have in mind, what kind and approximate amount of data would you use, and what would make the trial worthwhile?
- **Who would take part?** Roughly how many people are involved, and who would coordinate on your side?
- **What resources do you have?** Discuss available computers, storage, internet access, time, and any setup help needed.
- **What needs to stay private?** Understand what can be shared and bring unresolved questions to the technical team. Keeping raw data on someone’s computer does not by itself guarantee privacy.

For partners bringing a training project, share [What can I train on DIN?](../Documentation/public/what-can-be-trained.md).

The first conversation should give us enough information to suggest a useful next step. Detailed technical questions can follow during onboarding.

## Agree on the next step

Before wrapping up, confirm:

- Who follows up with the partner.
- Who leads the onboarding call.
- Who helps with setup, including a wallet, test ETH, staking where needed, and IPFS storage.
- What happens next, who will do it, and by when.

One person may cover several responsibilities. OPS-1 identifies Umer for technical onboarding calls and Operations/Interns for outreach; confirm availability for each partner.

## Question for discussion

How could we keep one shared record of partner conversations so we know who is following up and what happens next? One option could be a GitHub Discussion with a short entry for each partner. Would that work for the team, or would another approach be easier to maintain?
